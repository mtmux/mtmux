// use cases

The work doesn't stop when you leave the desk.

Six situations mtmux was built around. If none of them look like your week, you probably don't need it.

01 — UNATTENDED AGENTS

A 40-minute refactor you didn't watch

Agents don't fail loudly. They finish quietly, or wait forever on a permission dialog you'd have answered in a second.

mtmux pushes on done, blocked, stalled and failed — and lets you answer y/n from the lock screen.

02 — ON CALL

Triage before you find a laptop

The first five minutes of an incident are k9s, logs and one restart. That fits on a phone.

Your jump-host session is already open, with the dashboards you left running. No key on the phone, no VPN to negotiate at 2am.

03 — COMMUTING

Twenty minutes of real progress

Read the diff the agent produced, kick off the next task, watch tests, tweak a config line.

Buffered replay means tunnels cost you scroll position, not output.

04 — TRAVELLING LIGHT

Tablet, keyboard, no laptop

Tablet mode keeps real splits and passes Bluetooth chords straight through, prefix key included.

Your workstation does the compiling; the tablet is a very good terminal.

05 — LONG JOBS

Training runs and migrations

A six-hour job needs two things: a glance at the loss curve, and a shout if it dies.

Wrap it in mtmux run and check the pane from wherever you are — sixel plots render as-is.

06 — SHOWING SOMEONE

Read-only, time-limited

Sometimes a colleague needs to see the failure, not run commands in your shell.

mtmux share work:1 --ttl 1h mints a view link that expires on its own.

Try it on today's session.

Nothing to migrate, nothing to configure. If it isn't useful in a day, mtmux down and it's gone.
